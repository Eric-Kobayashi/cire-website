FOUR PATHS
01

Pair with Research Scientists

We work with research funding bodies and start the collaboration with the principal investigator from the proposal phase — so engineering is designed into the project, not bolted on after the grant lands.

Science once lived as a side project of people who could afford it. Scale-up made it a profession: principal investigators, grants, publications, institutions. That access came with overhead. Groups pay for the cost of distributing resources; PIs are pushed onto the grant treadmill; operational work crowds out the thinking the grant was meant to buy.

Scale-up is also a genuine instrument of discovery. At the Wellcome Sanger Institute, a single sequencing machine can now generate what the Human Genome Project took more than a decade, billions of dollars, and multinational cooperation to complete. More data lets rare events and faint patterns surface. Genome-wide association studies followed; gene therapy became conceivable.

Industry scales to ship a product. Science scales to see. Upscale engineering in industry is like building an apartment block, then fitting each floor to the market. In research it is like building a TV tower, dismantling it for a pyramid, then a treehouse. Each iteration adds understanding. Few components reuse. Scientists therefore need bespoke solutions — and they still write most of the software themselves.

Why not the support models already in place?

IT support answers across many groups. It does not embed, cannot prioritise a single project, and burns time on context switching.

Staff scientists cost the team continuously, often drift into admin, and cannot pause when the experiment is waiting on data.

Data scientists focus on the analysis the scientist wants to do. There is little complementary synergy.

We pair vertically: one research engineer, dedicated to the group, focused on the journey of the project — an engineering caretaker, not a ticket queue.

02

CTO in a Box

We provide “CTO in a box” for startups and focused research organisations. We work with venture capitalists — Cire is a distributor of venture human intelligence.

Think of a fund that invests people, not capital: research engineers placed into biotech startups and focused labs that cannot yet hire a full technical leadership bench.

As AI automates mundane office work, a surplus of human intellect becomes available. Many of those people already wanted to do research. They were kept out by the usual barriers:

  • Years of training before a topic can even be entered
  • Pay and posts that cannot compete with other sectors
  • A solitary culture scored by publication, not by how the work is built

Research engineering is the alternative path: a doctoral-level scientist who is computationally fluent, interested in translation, and looking for leadership through method rather than through a PI chair. Cire gathers them and deploys them where discovery is happening.

Science explores many solutions with versatility. Engineering commits to one robust, scalable design. A startup needs both — and it does not need another point solution. Successful work has to fit staff time, data infrastructure, procurement, regulation, and incentives, or it will not scale.

We focus first on scientific problems that can be applied and monetised. The long-term aim is a balanced portfolio of applied and fundamental research, as universities run: applied work pays the present; fundamental results wait in the literature like a bottle on the water, to be picked up when the time comes.

Do not sell another tool. Put a research engineer in the room.

03

Infrastructure for Platform Suppliers

We provide infrastructure implementation for platform suppliers expanding into scientific research — AI model providers such as OpenAI, cloud computing such as AWS.

We do not lack new tools. We lack specialised engineers with the human capacity to operate them in scientific settings.

Computational know-how lives on islands: the original author, the advanced user, the lab that happened to get a pipeline working. The barrier to sharing that knowledge is high. Future compute, datasets, and methods will concentrate further unless someone ports them into the place science actually happens.

Cire implements the last mile — the scientific application layer — so a general platform can survive the TV-tower-then-pyramid-then-treehouse cycle of a research programme, where each iteration adds understanding and few components reuse cleanly.

The aim is to make research software AI-FAIR: findable, and machine-readable by AI agents, so platforms and labs can meet without each group reverse-engineering the last mile.

Platforms supply the capability. Research engineers make it run in the lab.

04

Digital Twins of the Workflow

Through collaborative work with research groups, Cire accumulates hands-on experience of operating complex scientific analysis workflows. We distill that tacit knowledge into an AI knowledge base for each tool.

These base wrappers interface with both AI agents and human operators. They act like digital twins of the original author or advanced user — experts in a specific scientific analysis pipeline that never retire, and that keep evolving with their human engineer.

What do language models actually give the pair?

Speed to prototype, and a way to ask for unfamiliar scientific background.

They do not replace the pair. The engineer verifies the code; the scientist verifies the knowledge. The model is a tool, not a partner. A research engineer also absorbs the mental burden of an unfamiliar medium — composing the prompt, catching when the model misses the intention — so friction does not push the scientist away from the tool.

At the end of an engagement, the record of work with agents becomes a knowledge base the group can keep. Hard problems can draw on the combined expertise of every research engineer in Cire.

The wrappers are digital twins. The partnership is human.
